deki-physics-2d

2D 物理。剛体、箱・円・カプセル・多角形のコライダー、トリガー、レイキャスト、ヒンジ、ロープを備え、固定ステップで進むので、ゲームはどのフレームレートでも同じように動きます。

リポジトリ: github.com/dekiengine/deki-physics-2d · 最新リリース: 0.40.0 · エンジン 0.40.0 以降が必要

DekiEditor --packages-add deki-physics-2d <project> か、パッケージマネージャーからインストールします。エンジン自身のコンポーネントと C++ API は Deki のドキュメントにあります。

エディターでは Physics 2D と表示されます。

変更履歴

変更履歴は英語です。

0.40.0

Added

  • The package: rigid bodies that move and turn, box, circle, capsule and convex polygon colliders, triggers, layers, raycasts and collision and trigger callbacks, on a fixed step so a game plays the same at any frame rate.
  • A body falls, balances and spins about the centre of its colliders, wherever its object's origin is.
  • Hinge joints with limits and a motor, and distance joints and ropes.
  • Bodies at rest sleep, with everything touching them, until something wakes them.
  • Fast bodies stop at what they would otherwise jump over in one step.
  • A collider with no Rigidbody 2D follows its object, and a body follows an object that other code moves.
  • Nothing is allocated while a scene runs, once its lists have reached their size.
  • Colliders and joints are drawn in the Scene View, with handles to drag a selected collider's size and corners.
  • A world at rest is not stepped. With no body awake or moving and nothing changed from outside since the last step, a step returns at once; it used to find every contact again and copy what it kept of each. A settled pile of 100 boxes went from 22.7 to 0.2 microseconds a step on a desktop, and ends where it did before, bit for bit. Whatever code changes while the world rests (a push, a body or a trigger removed) is still stepped.
  • A contact keeps its two bodies and the friction between them for the step: each solver pass looked the shapes up again and took a square root.
  • A body asleep, or moved by code and not spinning, is not given its sine and cosine again every step.
  • A contact's two points are solved together. Solved one after the other, a box set down flat rocked from corner to corner, and a stack leant and fell.
  • A bounce gives back the speed the two arrived with, before the step's gravity. A ball that keeps all its speed rose 4% higher with every bounce; it now comes back to where it fell from.
  • A step is solved in sub-steps (World::subSteps, 4): each moves the bodies a little and solves from where they then are, so the weight of the top of a stack reaches the ground within the step. Several passes over one whole step carried it a box a pass, and a stack of more than ten stood or fell by the rounding of a sum: twenty boxes stood on a desktop and fell on an ESP32-S3. Now ten stand however they are set down, twenty with six sub-steps, the same on both.
  • An overlap is pushed out as a spring would, and the speed that takes is taken back in the same sub-step, so it moves the bodies and adds no energy. Bodies rest 2.5 to 5 mm into each other; a stack of 20 went to sleep 16 mm into each box below.
  • Shapes within 5 mm of each other already have a contact, which lets them close the gap and no more. Boxes set down exactly touching had none until they had sunk into each other, a box a step: the top of a stack of 20 fell half a metre. Nothing is reported, and a trigger is not entered, until two shapes overlap.
  • A joint is set up from where its bodies are in each sub-step. A pendulum keeps 97% of its swing over 20 seconds; it kept 90%.
  • A step costs less than it did before sub-steps. On a T-Deck 30 boxes awake take 1.6 ms a step (1.7 before), 10 take 0.35 ms and a chain of 30 hinges 0.8 ms (0.9). A pass works on a contact point as four numbers instead of two arms; a body keeps what its contacts hold it with, so a sub-step does not go over the contacts to start from it; last step's contacts are found through a sorted list of their pairs instead of being sorted themselves; a face the other shape is plainly behind is not measured; and no pass divides, which a processor without a divide instruction does slowly.
